# Composition and multiple views

Use this reference when arrangement changes what can be inferred, when the explanation contains several views or accounts, or when learning and lookup place competing demands on the layout. Describe the intended information arrangement without prescribing coordinates or a renderer.

## Lay out the work the reader must do

Start with the question and trace a plausible route to its answer. Identify the entry point, evidence needed for the next inference, and where a qualification changes that inference. A reading path is useful when it reduces search; it need not be a forced numbered sequence.

For comparisons, align the dimensions being compared. Preserve units, item identity, and relevant scale correspondence. For lookup, give readers a stable key and grouping rule. A dense, indexed reference can serve lookup well even if it would make a poor introductory story.

Determine what can move to a secondary view by testing the primary view on its own. If removing an exception, denominator, uncertainty, or competing explanation makes its headline false, that material belongs in the primary reading path. Supporting derivations can be secondary when the qualification they justify remains visible.

## Make boundaries and emphasis carry explicit meanings

Decide whether a group means membership, location, responsibility, a common stage, or merely a set of alternatives. Use a heading, alignment, enclosure, or proximity that communicates that intended relationship. Avoid enclosures that accidentally claim exclusive ownership or physical containment.

Nearby elements are often read together. If an important relation spans distant areas, specify its connection or correspondence instead of hoping the reader will match it. Conversely, drawing every connection can obscure the relevant structure. Preserve the relationships needed for the task, not every possible association.

Assign roles to emphasis. A highlighted label can identify the takeaway without increasing a quantitative mark's encoded size. An uncertain claim may deserve attention without appearing better supported than the alternatives. Similar prominence is appropriate when sources genuinely disagree and no supported ranking exists.

Use repeated labels when they prevent identity loss across panels, page turns, or arbitrary lookup entry points. Remove repetition that merely restates surrounding prose, but do not equate less text with less work for the reader.

## Choose among one view, several views, and progressive detail

| Arrangement | Useful when | Cost to address |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One integrated view | The inference depends on simultaneous connections or comparisons. | Crossings, label competition, and mixed scales may need restructuring. |
| Side-by-side views | Corresponding entities or alternatives need direct comparison. | Stable names, ordering, legends, and appropriate scale correspondence. |
| Overview plus detail | The reader needs both global context and a local mechanism. | Make the expanded region identifiable and preserve relevant external links. |
| Sequential views | Change or a causal sequence is the subject. | Preserve orientation and identity; explain omitted intermediate states. |
| Interactive disclosure | Readers choose among substantial optional details. | The initial view must remain truthful; essential comparisons cannot require remembering hidden values. |

Use only as many views as the questions warrant. No universal node count determines when to split. Expertise, available space, labels, task, and connection structure all affect whether a split helps.

## Separate changes in the subject from changes in the drawing

For a before/after comparison, name the snapshots and establish which entities correspond. Distinguish a retained entity with changed attributes from a replacement; a rename or a new position on the page does not settle identity. For schematic connections, describe changes through their endpoints and meaning rather than incidental bends. If path geometry encodes a physical route or another consequential property, preserve that information too. Include concise old/new descriptions for consequential differences rather than relying on absence or color alone.

Keep unaffected context recognizable where that helps comparison, but do not require identical coordinates. When repositioning is merely a layout choice, avoid presenting it as physical relocation or changed responsibility. If a component is absent from a later view, establish whether it was removed from the subject or simply omitted from that view. With incomplete snapshots, leave that difference unresolved.

For example, an invented lending network retains branches East and West, but redirects East's returns from depot A to depot B. Preserve the branch identities and state the changed destination. Depot A's absence from a later cropped diagram does not establish that it closed. If the only change is East's opening hours, a short attribute comparison may answer the question without two network views.

Even when snapshots establish a change, they do not by themselves establish the intermediate steps, migration order, downtime, or reason for it. Supply those only when the task needs them and the source supports them.

## Worked example: two incompatible accounts

Two invented records describe the same manuscript. **Catalog A** says it was donated in 1930. **Letter B** says it was purchased in 1934. The supplied material does not reconcile them.

A source-faithful composition can place the manuscript identity above two parallel account panels, each containing the attributed claim and its date. A shared statement says “Acquisition history unresolved in the supplied records.” This lets the reader compare the disagreement without converting it into one chronological story.

Do not connect “donated in 1930” to “purchased in 1934” as successive established events. Do not privilege one account through a “correct answer” highlight absent supporting evidence. If a later source resolves the discrepancy, the composition can change accordingly.

Counterexample to always placing everything side by side: a long reference catalog used to retrieve one known manuscript may work better as indexed entries. Simultaneous comparison is useful when comparison is the task, not as a universal page pattern.

## Handoff checks

Specify what each view answers, how readers identify the same entity across views, and which information stays together. Check the headline without its footnotes, then check a secondary view without relying on undocumented context. Neither should invite a materially false inference.

For a requested narrow or static version, describe how the information relationship survives the changed arrangement. Reflow may turn columns into sections; it must preserve which evidence supports which claim. Whether actual typography and geometry succeed belongs to rendered inspection.

## Basis and limits

- [Larkin and Simon](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1551-6708.1987.tb00863.x) analyze locality and information search in computational representations; this is not a measured cognitive-load score for a proposed layout.
- [Green and Blackwell's Cognitive Dimensions tutorial](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~afb21/CognitiveDimensions/CDtutorial.pdf) discusses visibility, juxtaposition, and tradeoffs among activities.
- [Impeccable's layout guidance](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable/blob/9d715cc4f5564a990ca8345abfdd5df6dc9b41c8/skill/reference/layout.md) is practitioner inspiration for task-sensitive composition. Its frontend production workflow is not required here.

The arrangement choices and manuscript example are original applications. Predicted ease of reading remains a design hypothesis until tested with readers.
